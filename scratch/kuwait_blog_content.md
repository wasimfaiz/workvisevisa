## Legal Framework: Understanding the Article 18 Private Sector Work Visa

Under the Foreigners Residence Law (Decree Law No. 17/1959) and its executive regulations, residency visas in Kuwait are categorized into distinct statutory "Articles":
- **Article 17:** Public sector / Government ministry employment.
- **Article 18 (*Mada 18*):** Private sector contractual employment (commercial companies, contracting enterprises, industrial plants, hospitals, hotels, and logistics).
- **Article 20 (*Mada 20*):** Domestic workers (house drivers, cooks, domestic domestic helpers under individual citizen sponsorship).
- **Article 22 (*Mada 22*):** Family / Dependent residency (spouse and minor children).

### Key Statutory Provisions of the Article 18 Work Visa:
1. **The Electronic Work Permit (*Izn Amal - إذن عمل*)**: Before an entry visa can be issued, the sponsoring Kuwaiti company must apply for an initial work permit through PAM's digital **Ashal** or **Sahel Business** portal. PAM verifies that the company has valid government labor quota files (*Milaf*), active commercial licenses with MOCI, and zero unpaid wage complaints on the Wage Protection System (WPS).
2. **The Ministry of Interior Entry Visa Slip (*Simat Dukhool - سمة دخول*)**: Once PAM approves the work permit, the file transfers automatically to the General Directorate of Residency under the Ministry of Interior. The MOI issues the official pink/blue entry visa slip bearing the candidate's passport details and sponsoring company code.
3. **Statutory Validity**: The entry visa allows the worker **90 days** from the date of issue to enter Kuwait. Once inside the country, the employer must complete medical and fingerprint formalities to stamp the multi-year **Article 18 Residence Permit (Iqama)** into the passport and issue the **Civil ID Card**, typically valid for **1, 2, or 3 years** (renewable).
4. **Statutory Prohibition on Visa Trading**: Under Article 10 of Kuwait Labor Law No. 6/2010, employers are strictly prohibited from recruiting foreign workers and leaving them unemployed or charging them any fees for visa issuance. Violators face imprisonment of up to 3 years and fines up to KD 10,000 per worker.

## Critical Anti-Scam Advisory: 6 Red Flags of Kuwait Visa Fraud

Because Kuwait offers the highest currency value in the world, fraudulent operators prey aggressively on jobseekers across rural Bihar, Eastern UP, and Rajasthan. Protect your family's life savings by recognizing these 6 scams:

### 1. The "Azad Visa / Open Visa" Illusion
**The Trap:** An agent promises: *"Bhaiya, ₹2.5 Lakh de do, Kuwait ka Azad Visa 18 dilwa denge. Wahan jakar kisi bhi company mein apni marzi se kaam kar lena."*
**The Ground Reality:** **100% ILLEGAL UNDER KUWAIT LAW.** There is no such legal category as an "Azad Visa" in Kuwait. Sponsoring companies that issue visas and let foreign workers labor for third parties violate **Article 10 of Kuwait Labor Law No. 6/2010** and residency laws. 
The Ministry of Interior conducts continuous inspection raids in industrial and commercial areas (Shuwaikh, Jleeb Al-Shuyoukh, Mahboula, Khaitan). Any worker caught working for a sponsor other than the one named on their Civil ID is immediately arrested, sent to the **Talha Deportation Center**, and permanently banned from entering all GCC countries.

### 2. Fake Paper Visas Issued by "Ghost / Shell Companies"
**The Trap:** Scammers show you a printed paper visa with Arabic script and stamps, demanding full payment before travel.
**The Ground Reality:** Corrupt brokers register fake paper shell companies (*Sharikat Wahmiya*) with no real projects. They sell visas to Indian workers, but by the time the worker lands, PAM has already blacklisted the company code for non-compliance. The worker is refused entry at Kuwait airport or stranded without salary, housing, or residency. 
Always verify that your sponsoring employer has an active commercial license on the **Ministry of Commerce & Industry portal ([moci.gov.kw](https://www.moci.gov.kw))** and verified project contracts.

### 3. The Article 20 (Domestic Servant) to Article 18 Bait-and-Switch
**The Trap:** An agent tells you, *"Pehle domestic driver ya houseboy ka Article 20 visa le lo, Kuwait pahunch kar company 18 visa mein convert karwa degi."*
**The Ground Reality:** The Kuwait Ministry of Interior enforces strict, near-total bans on converting domestic helper visas (Article 20) to private sector commercial work permits (Article 18). You will remain trapped under individual domestic sponsor control, earning less than half the commercial wage, with zero labor court protections.

### 4. Counterfeit Kuwait Medical Fit Slips from Unlicensed Clinics
Only medical examinations conducted through diagnostic clinics authorized by the **Wafid / GAMCA Electronic Portal ([wafid.com](https://wafid.com))** are accepted by the Embassy of Kuwait. Any agent who claims they can "manage a medical fit certificate without you visiting an authorized center" is issuing a forged certificate that will be detected during VFS submission, leading to an immediate visa ban.

### 5. Outrageously Exaggerated Salaries for Unskilled Labor
If an agent promises an unskilled general cleaner or agricultural worker basic salary of **KD 400 to KD 500 (₹1,10,000 to ₹1,40,000 INR)** with "no interview and free air tickets," you are dealing with advance-fee fraud. As proven by official wage tables, standard entry-level unskilled wages range between **KD 110 and KD 140 (₹38,500 to ₹52,500 INR)**. High salaries in Kuwait are reserved for certified technical trades (6G welders, heavy crane operators, HVAC technicians).

Scammers construct fake websites mimicking Kuwaiti ministries (e.g., `moi-kuwait-visa-check.com` or `kuwait-visa-online.org`) to show fake "Approved" statuses.
