## Legal Framework: The Qiwa Digital Contract & The Modern Iqama System

Under the historic **Labor Reform Initiative (LRI)** enacted by the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development (MHRSD), the traditional "Kafala" sponsorship model was fundamentally restructured into a legally binding, digitally authenticated bilateral employment contract.

### 1. The Qiwa Digital Employment Contract (*Al-Aqd Al-Muwaththaq*)
- **Mandatory Electronic Authentication:** No Saudi work visa can be processed without a digitally authenticated contract registered on the official **Qiwa Platform (`qiwa.sa`)**.
- **Transparency of Terms:** The digital contract explicitly details the candidate's exact occupational title, basic monthly wage in SAR, food and housing allowances, standard working hours (8 hours/day, 48 hours/week), probation period (maximum 90 days, extendable to 180 days by mutual written consent), and paid annual leave (minimum 21 days for the first 5 years, 30 days thereafter).
- **Candidate Digital Verification:** The worker receives an SMS notification and can review the entire contract text (available in both Arabic and English) before giving their electronic acceptance.

### 2. The Biometric Residence Permit (Iqama - إقامة)
- **Legal Residency Document:** The **Muqeem Iqama** is a plastic, credit-card-sized biometric identity card issued by the General Directorate of Passports (**Jawazat / الجوازات**) under the Ministry of Interior.
- **Issuance Timeline:** By law, the sponsoring employer must complete medical registration, health insurance enrollment, and Iqama issuance within **90 days of the worker's arrival in Saudi Arabia**. Failure to do so incurs statutory penalties of SAR 500 to SAR 1,000 on the employer.
- **Statutory Employee Protections:**
  - **No Passport Confiscation:** Under Council of Ministers Decision No. 166, employers are strictly prohibited from retaining workers' passports. The passport must remain in the physical custody of the employee at all times. Violators face immediate fines of SAR 2,000 to SAR 10,000 per violation.
  - **Free Mobility (Naql Kafala):** If an employer fails to pay salaries for three consecutive months, fails to issue the Iqama within 90 days, or if the contract term expires, the worker has the absolute legal right to transfer to any other licensed company in Saudi Arabia through Qiwa **without the current sponsor's permission**.

## Critical Anti-Scam Advisory: 6 Red Flags of Saudi Visa Fraud

Because Saudi Arabia is the largest overseas employment destination for Indian workers, fraudulent operators prey aggressively on jobseekers across rural Bihar, Eastern UP, Rajasthan, and Punjab. Protect your family's life savings by recognizing these 6 scams:

### 1. The "Azad Visa / Free Visa" Trap
**The Trap:** An agent promises: *"Bhaiya, ₹2 Lakh de do, Saudi ka Azad Visa dilwa denge. Wahan jakar kisi bhi company mein apni marzi se kaam kar lena, koi rok-tok nahi hogi."*
**The Ground Reality:** **100% ILLEGAL UNDER SAUDI LAW.** Under **Article 39 of the Saudi Labor Law**, it is a severe criminal offense for a foreign employee to work for any employer other than their official sponsor (*Kafeel*), or to work as an independent freelancer (*Ta'shira Hurra*).
The Saudi Ministry of Interior conducts continuous, aggressive inspection campaigns across construction sites, workshops, and commercial markets. Foreign workers caught working on "Azad visas" face:
- Immediate arrest and detention in **Tarheel (Deportation Center)**.
- Fines of up to **SAR 50,000**.
- Administrative deportation and a **permanent lifetime entry ban across all GCC countries**.
Never pay for an "Azad Visa." Always demand a verified, employer-sponsored contract registered on Qiwa.

### 2. Fake Qiwa Contract Screenshots Created on Mobile Apps
**The Trap:** Scammers show you a mobile screenshot or PDF with the Qiwa logo, claiming that your electronic contract is approved, and demanding full payment before departure.
**The Ground Reality:** Real Qiwa contract verifications are performed directly by the worker by logging into the official portal **`qiwa.sa`** using their registered mobile number and passport details. If an agent refuses to provide the official contract link or claims "it is internal company confidential information," the contract is 100% counterfeit.

### 3. Fake Takamol SVP Certificates Sold Without Physical Testing
Any agent who claims they can "manage a Takamol SVP pass certificate without you visiting an authorized testing workshop" is issuing a forged certificate. Takamol certificates are verified digitally via QR codes linked directly to the sovereign **`svp.qiwa.sa`** database. The Saudi Embassy detects forged certificates during visa submission, resulting in immediate visa rejection and an international blacklist.

### 4. Sponsoring Companies Classified as "Red" on MHRSD Nitaqat
Before accepting any job offer, verify the sponsoring company's status on the **Nitaqat (Saudization)** scale. Companies classified in the "Red" (*Ahmar*) category are prohibited from recruiting foreign workers, renewing Iqamas, or transferring visas. Legitimate recruitment is conducted strictly with companies in the "Platinum" (*Balatini*) or "High Green" (*Akhdar Murtafi'*) categories.

### 5. Outrageously Exaggerated Salaries for Unskilled Labor
If an agent promises an unskilled general helper or cleaning job paying **SAR 4,500 to SAR 6,000 (₹1,00,000 to ₹1,38,000 INR)** with "no interview and free air tickets," you are dealing with advance-fee fraud. As proven by official wage indices, standard entry-level unskilled wages range between **SAR 1,100 and SAR 1,400 (₹25,000 to ₹32,000 INR)**. High salaries in KSA are reserved for certified technical trades (6G welders, heavy crane operators, HVAC technicians).

### 6. WhatsApp "Online Visa Verifications" on Cloned Domains
Scammers construct fake websites mimicking Saudi ministries to show fake "Approved" statuses. Real visa tracking is conducted strictly on the sovereign portal **[visa.mofa.gov.sa](https://visa.mofa.gov.sa)**. ### Q3: What is the difference between an Entry Visa and an Iqama?
- **Entry Visa (Ta'shirat Dukhool):** A visa sticker affixed to your passport by the Saudi Embassy in New Delhi or Consulate in Mumbai, valid for 90 days. Its sole purpose is to allow legal border entry into Saudi Arabia.
- **Iqama (Muqeem Residence Permit):** A biometric plastic identity card issued by the General Directorate of Passports (Jawazat) after you arrive in Saudi Arabia. It serves as your official legal resident identity, valid for 1 year (renewable).

### Q4: Can I change employers in Saudi Arabia without my sponsor's consent?
Yes. Under the Labor Reform Initiative (LRI), expatriate workers can transfer to a new employer through the **Qiwa portal** without sponsor consent if:
1. The original employment contract term has expired.
2. The current employer fails to pay wages for 3 consecutive months.
3. The current employer fails to issue or renew the Iqama within 90 days of arrival or expiry.
4. The company falls into the "Red" Nitaqat category.

### Q5: What is the summer midday work ban in Saudi Arabia?
Under Ministerial Decision No. 3337, the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development strictly prohibits outdoor work under direct sunlight from **12:00 PM to 3:00 PM** between **June 15 and September 15** every year. Employers who violate this ban face severe fines and temporary closure of project sites.

### Q6: Can an Indian worker obtain a Saudi Driving License?
Yes. Workers holding an authentic Indian Smart Card Driving License can drive during their initial months with an International Driving Permit (IDP). Once your Iqama is issued, you can register at an authorized Saudi driving school (such as Dallah Driving School), complete the theoretical computer exam and practical road driving test, and receive your 5-year or 10-year **Saudi Driving License (*Rukhsa Qiyada*)**.

### Q7: Can my family (wife and children) join me on a Family Visa in Saudi Arabia?
Under updated MHRSD regulations, expatriates holding professional, technical, or supervisory job designations with a minimum basic salary of **SAR 4,000 to SAR 5,000 per month** can sponsor their spouse and minor children for a **Permanent Family Residency Visa (*Iqama Istitlaq*)**. Expatriates must pay the statutory expat dependent levy (*Rusoom Al-Murafeeqeen* - SAR 400 per dependent per month).

### Q8: What are the standard working hours and overtime rules under Saudi Labor Law?
Under Article 98 and 107 of the Saudi Labor Law:
- Standard working hours are **8 hours per day** or **48 hours per week** (reduced to 6 hours/day or 36 hours/week during the Holy Month of Ramadan for Muslim workers).
- Overtime hours are compensated at **1.5 times (150%)** the basic hourly wage.

### Q9: What is the End of Service Award (*Mukaafaat Nihayat Al-Khidma*)?
Under Article 84 of the Saudi Labor Law, every employee is legally entitled to end-of-service severance pay upon contract completion:
- **First 5 Years of Service:** Half a month's wage for each completed year.
- **Subsequent Years (Beyond 5 Years):** One full month's wage for each completed year.
Severance pay is calculated on the basis of the last drawn basic salary plus standard fixed allowances.

### Q10: Is personal income subject to tax in Saudi Arabia?
**No.** The Kingdom of Saudi Arabia imposes **0% personal income tax** on salaries earned by foreign employees. What you earn in basic salary, overtime, and allowances is 100% tax-free.

### Q11: What healthcare protections do Indian workers receive in Saudi Arabia?
Under the Council of Cooperative Health Insurance (**CCHI**) regulations, sponsoring employers must provide comprehensive private health insurance coverage through accredited insurance providers (such as Bupa Arabia, Tawuniya, or Medgulf) from the day of arrival. This unlocks 100% free emergency medical care, specialized surgical interventions, and prescription medications across private hospital networks throughout KSA.

### Q12: Do I need an eMigrate clearance to travel to Saudi Arabia?
